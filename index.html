      Small number = how far you were from that note after allowing for your own tuning. Blue outline = outside the key (kept because you clearly sang it). Tap one to hear and select it. ✎ = edited, 〰 = vibrato kept.

      Detection settings

      Only decides near-ties: a note halfway between two semitones goes to the one in your key.

      If you slowly go sharp or flat (or start a phrase a little off), notes are read the way a listener hears them: in the key, relative to where your voice has drifted.

      Sticky ignores bigger wobbles; Loose catches very quick notes.

      Shortcuts & tips
      • Listen turns on the tuner and click without recording, so you can find your note first. ♪ Play my note (or N) plays the note you're closest to.
      • Roll: click to jump there; drag across to loop that part. Space = play / pause. Ctrl + scroll zooms the timeline; ⛶ = full screen.
      • Edit a note: drag the middle to move it, drag its ends to change length (hold Alt or Ctrl to place freely). Double-click empty space to add one. ↑ ↓ change pitch. Delete removes the note; Shift + Delete removes the note and the sound under it. Ctrl+Z undoes.
      • Cut a part out: drag across it to loop it, then ✂ Cut this part out — the sound and notes go, and the rest moves earlier.
      • Harmony: Shift + drag a note to drop a copy (vibrato and all). Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V copies the selected note to the playhead.
      • Quantize pulls notes toward the beat grid by the Humanize amount — move the lever after pressing it to adjust. Snap exactly puts them right on it. With a loop set, only that part changes.
      • Vibrato: a wavy line on a note = vibrato it found. Keep plays and exports it; V toggles it on the selected note.

      Why it hears notes better than a tuner

      A tuner judges one instant in absolute hertz, so mid-note it can flip to the neighbour. A listener does three other things.

      They calibrate to you. If your whole take sits 30¢ flat, they hear your notes relative to each other. This measures where your personal semitone grid sits, and follows it if you drift.

      They hear a note as one event. The scoop into it, the vibrato and the sag at the end average into one pitch; short slides fold into the note they lead to.

      They know the key. A pitch honestly between two notes is heard as the one that belongs to the tune. A clearly sung out-of-key note stays.

      Best results: one voice, quiet room, mic 20–40 cm away, a clear consonant ("da", "la") on repeated notes.
